How it works

  1. Upload a file. Its fingerprint is computed in your browser — the file's exact bytes, nothing altered, and nothing leaves your device for the computation.
  2. It comes back stamped. A small band on an added margin carries the identifier; the original bytes stay untouched and separately identified.
  3. It's registered. The identifier gets a position in a public, exportable registry — and a verification page anyone can check.

What an AXN does not claim

Identity ≠ authorship — it proves a file, not who made it; authorship statements are recorded as declarations and marked as such.
Witnessing ≠ notarization — timestamps prove when a file was witnessed, not created; nothing here is legal advice.
A registry entry ≠ perpetual hosting — storage terms are always explicit.

If we disappear

Identifiers stay valid, because they're computed from your file — not granted by us. The registry lives in public version control with full history, and if this service ever ends, the whole archive is offered for download during a stated wind-down. The constitution spells it out.
