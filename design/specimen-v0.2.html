SIG · 0  COVER — THE LAWS OF THIS LANGUAGE

00 · Design laws — stated once, enforced everywhere

Color is data. A hue may appear only when it names a byte-cluster. Two constants: rubric crimson (= SIGNAL) and seal gold (= ARCHITECTURAL, constitutional authority only).
The hash face is the headline face. One family, three voices: Plex Mono (display & data), Plex Sans (interface), Plex Serif (constitution & plate captions only).
One mechanical interaction. The symbolon halves meeting. Everything else stays still.
No social proof, no gradients, no rounded corners, no generated mysticism. Real holdings are the only imagery.
Stamps live on paper. The band renders on light ground; the registry on sealed dark. The contrast is the material truth of the system.
No reader is observed. Production self-hosts fonts and assets; zero external calls. The archive's standard, kept on its own door.
Errors are corrected by amendment and tombstone, in the interface as in the registry. Nothing is silently deleted, including design decisions.

III

ARTICLE III · CONTINUITY

Ratified by the archive's editorial authority and published against the day no one asks permission.

Identifiers stay valid because they are computed from the file, not granted by this service. The registry lives in public version control with full history. If this service ends, the whole archive is offered for download during a stated wind-down.

What an AXN does not claim

Identity ≠ authorship. It proves a file, not who made it.

Witnessing ≠ notarization. Timestamps prove when a file was witnessed, not created.

A registry entry ≠ perpetual hosting. Storage terms are always explicit.

The correction clause

Errors are corrected by amendment and tombstone — never by silent deletion. A retired position keeps resolving; a withdrawn work is marked, not erased. The registry's history is itself part of the registry.
